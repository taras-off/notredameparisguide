Most people "do" Notre-Dame in about twenty minutes. They cross the square, photograph the facade, look up at the vault and leave — having seen one of the greatest buildings in Europe without ever being told what they were looking at. Without a guide you see stone. With one, you understand what happened here: who was crowned under this roof, who was shut out, what nearly destroyed it, and what the carvings over the doors were meant to say.

That's what the TouringBee audio guide is for. It's a self-guided tour on your own phone that begins on the square in front of the cathedral and takes you all the way through the interior — told as stories, not as a list of dates.

Start in the queue, finish inside

This is the part most visitors get wrong: they think the visit begins at the door. In fact a big share of Notre-Dame's best material is on the outside — the gallery of kings across the facade, the three great portals carved like a stone comic strip, the rose window, the towers, the gargoyles. And all of it can be seen from the square, which is free and open, with no security line.

So the wait to get in stops being dead time. You put your headphones on in the queue, work through the first stops while the line moves, and walk in already understanding half the building. If you're asked to take your headphones off at security, you put them back on inside and carry on exactly where you stopped. And you don't need a signal — the whole tour is already on your phone.

The stories you'll actually hear

We won't give away the endings — half the pleasure is hearing them in front of the exact stone they belong to — but here's a taste of the 31 stops:

  • Why a king of England was once crowned under this roof — and why it could only ever happen once.
  • The royal groom who married at Notre-Dame but, because of his faith, wasn't allowed inside for his own wedding.
  • The copper marker on the square that's said to bring you back to Paris — and the small ritual that goes with it.
  • How to tell a gargoyle from a chimera, and what the gargoyles are really there for (it's more plumbing than demons).
  • The vanished Court of Miracles — the lawless beggars' quarter Victor Hugo made famous.
  • The statue on the facade that carries its own head in its hands, and the saint behind that very Parisian image.

Every story is tied to something you can see, so you're never listening to trivia in the abstract — you're looking straight at it.

How it works

You buy the tour, get an activation email within a few minutes and install the free TouringBee app for iPhone or Android. Download your language once — hotel Wi-Fi is perfect — and from then on everything works fully offline, including the map. No roaming, no dropped signal behind thick stone walls.
