Most people "do" Notre-Dame in about twenty minutes. They cross the square, photograph the facade, step inside, look up at the vault, and leave — having seen one of the greatest buildings in Europe without ever being told what they were looking at. The stone is spectacular. The story behind it is better. And the story is the part nobody hands you at the door.

That's the gap the TouringBee Notre-Dame audio guide is built to fill. It's a self-guided tour you run from your own phone — 31 stops, about two hours, narrated by a professional historian, that starts out on the square in front of the cathedral and walks you all the way through to the far end of the interior. Not a list of dates. Stories: who was crowned here, who was shut out, what nearly destroyed the building, and what the carvings over the doors were really meant to say.

This page is the honest rundown of what the guide is, what you'll hear, how it works on the day, and the exclusive price for readers of this site. If you already know you want it, you can start here.

A king of England was crowned under this roof

Here's the kind of thing you walk straight past without the guide. Notre-Dame has seen coronations, a royal wedding where the groom wasn't allowed inside, emperors, revolutions and one very famous novel that saved the building from demolition. The audio guide doesn't just name these events — it stops you in the exact spot where each one happened and tells you the story while you're standing in it.

Stand in front of the cathedral and you're on ground that has been the stage for Paris for two thousand years, from a Roman town to a medieval capital. The guide opens right there, on the square, and the first thing it does is make the building make sense — why it's this big, why it was built here, and what stood on the spot before it.

Why the outside matters as much as the inside — and how it saves you the queue

This is the practical reason the tour is built the way it is. Roughly the first third of Notre-Dame's best material is on the outside: the two towers, the gallery of kings across the facade, the three great portals carved like a stone comic strip, the gargoyles, the rose window, the spire. You can take all of that in from the square — and the square is free and open, with no security line.

So if there's a wait to get inside, it isn't dead time. You start the audio guide outside, work through the facade and the forecourt while the queue moves, and walk in already understanding half the building. If you're asked to take your headphones off going through the entrance, you simply put them back on once you're through and pick up where you left off. By the time you're inside, you're not a tourist glancing around — you know exactly what you're looking for.

Written by a historian, not copied off a plaque

There's a real difference between a tour that reads you facts and one that was actually written by someone who knows the material. This one was put together by a professional historian, and it shows in what it chooses to tell you. It's reliable — the history is accurate — but it's not academic. No endless dates, no jargon, no "constructed in the Rayonnant style of the High Gothic period." Instead it reads the building the way medieval Parisians did.

Because that's the secret of a Gothic cathedral: almost every carving is there to be read. The figures over the doors, the scenes in the stained glass, the kings along the facade — for a largely illiterate city, this was a book in stone, the "poor man's bible." The guide teaches you to read it, so a wall of carvings turns into actual stories you can follow with your own eyes.

The stories you'll actually hear

We're not going to give away the endings — half the pleasure is hearing them on the spot — but here's a taste of what's waiting across the 31 stops:

  • The copper marker in the square that's said to bring you back to Paris — and the small ritual the guide tells you to perform on it.
  • The royal groom who married at Notre-Dame but, because of his faith, wasn't allowed to set foot inside for his own wedding.
  • Why a king of England was once crowned here — and why it could only ever happen once.
  • How to tell a gargoyle from a chimera, and what the gargoyles are honestly there to do (it's more plumbing than demons).
  • The vanished "Court of Miracles" — the lawless beggars' quarter near the cathedral that Victor Hugo made famous.
  • The statue on the facade that carries its own head in its hands, and the saint behind that very Parisian image.

Each one is tied to a place you can see, so you're never listening to trivia in the abstract — you're looking straight at the thing being described.

How it works: download once, then one tap at each stop

It's simpler than it sounds. You buy the tour, get an email with activation instructions within a few minutes, and download the free TouringBee app for iPhone or Android. Before you go — on hotel Wi-Fi is perfect — you download your language, and from then on the whole thing works completely offline. No roaming, no dropped signal inside the thick stone walls.

On the day, the app shows an offline map with the route and the 31 numbered stops. You walk to a spot, tap its number, and the historian starts talking — illustrations on screen help you pick out exactly which carving or window is meant. There's no group and no schedule: you set the pace, pause for photos, replay the bits you liked, and the tour stays yours for a full year in the language you chose. All you bring is your phone and a pair of headphones.

No — it's an independent self-guided tour by TouringBee, written by a professional historian. That's the point of it: it's built to be told as stories, inside and out, in 10 languages, and it stays on your phone for a year.
